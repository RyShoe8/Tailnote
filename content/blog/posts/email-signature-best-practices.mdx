Every email your team sends is a brand impression. For solo founders and small teams, a sloppy signature undermines trust faster than a typo in the subject line.

This guide covers what actually matters — and what you can safely skip.

## Keep it scannable

Recipients scan signatures in under two seconds. Lead with name and role, then one contact method, then optional links.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The 4-line rule">
  Name, title, company, one phone or email — that's enough for most SMB outbound. Everything else is optional.
</Callout>

Avoid stacking ten social icons, three disclaimers, and a banner ad. Gmail and Outlook clip long signatures anyway.

## One source of truth

When everyone maintains their own signature, drift is inevitable. Marketing updates the logo; sales still uses last year's version.

Centralize signature content in a tool or template your team can copy from — not a Word doc buried in Google Drive.

## Images that survive real inboxes

Hosted logos beat pasted images. Use PNG with a sensible width (120–200px), link to your site, and always include `alt` text.

```html
<a href="https://tailnote.io">
  <img src="https://tailnote.io/email-assets/logo.png" alt="Tailnote" width="140" height="40" />
</a>
```

## Legal disclaimers: less is more

Lengthy legal blocks push reply content below the fold on mobile. If you need a disclaimer, keep it to two lines and link to full terms on your website.

## Promotional blocks without spam vibes

A single, relevant CTA — book a call, read a case study, register for a webinar — can outperform a full marketing footer. Rotate offers org-wide so updates happen once, not in twenty inboxes.
