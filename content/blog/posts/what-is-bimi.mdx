BIMI (Brand Indicators for Message Identification) lets supported email clients show your verified logo next to messages in the inbox. It's the closest thing email has to a blue checkmark for brand recognition.

## Prerequisites you can't skip

BIMI isn't a standalone fix. You need:

1. **DMARC at enforcement** — `p=quarantine` or `p=reject`
2. **SPF and DKIM passing** for all legitimate senders
3. A **verified logo** in SVG Tiny P/S format
4. For Gmail: a **Verified Mark Certificate (VMC)** from an approved provider

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't rush BIMI">
  If your DMARC policy is still `p=none`, fix authentication first. BIMI without strong DMARC gives you a logo on mail that might still be spoofable.
</Callout>

## What recipients actually see

In Gmail, Yahoo, and Apple Mail (with varying support), authenticated messages from BIMI-enabled domains can display your logo in the avatar slot — replacing the generic initial circle.

That visual consistency reinforces trust, especially for sales and support teams sending high-volume outbound.

## Is BIMI worth it for a team of 25?

**Probably not on day one.** The VMC alone can cost hundreds per year, and setup time is non-trivial.

Prioritize instead:

- Consistent [email signatures](/blog/email-signature-best-practices) with a hosted logo
- [SPF, DKIM, and DMARC](/blog/spf-dkim-dmarc-explained) at enforcement
- A clean sending reputation

Revisit BIMI once you're sending significant volume and want every inbox touchpoint branded — including the message list view.

## BIMI DNS record (when you're ready)

```txt
default._bimi.yourdomain.com TXT "v=BIMI1; l=https://yourdomain.com/logo.svg;"
```

With VMC for Gmail:

```txt
default._bimi.yourdomain.com TXT "v=BIMI1; l=https://yourdomain.com/logo.svg; a=https://yourdomain.com/vmc.pem;"
```
