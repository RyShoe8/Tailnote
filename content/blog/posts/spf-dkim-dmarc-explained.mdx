If you've ever wondered why your newsletters land in spam — or why someone can fake emails from your domain — the answer usually lives in three acronyms: **SPF**, **DKIM**, and **DMARC**.

## SPF — who can send as you?

SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a DNS TXT record listing which mail servers are allowed to send email for your domain.

```txt
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:sendgrid.net ~all
```

When a receiving server gets mail claiming to be from `@yourcompany.com`, it checks SPF. If the sending IP isn't authorized, the message may fail or get flagged.

<Callout variant="info" title="Common mistake">
  You can only have one SPF record per domain. If you use Google Workspace and a newsletter tool, merge both into a single `v=spf1` record — don't publish two separate TXT records.
</Callout>

## DKIM — cryptographic proof

DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) adds a digital signature to each outgoing message. Receiving servers verify the signature against a public key in your DNS.

Unlike SPF, DKIM survives forwarding — which makes it essential for B2B email where messages get relayed between help desks and shared inboxes.

## DMARC — the policy layer

DMARC tells receivers what to do when SPF or DKIM fails, and sends you aggregate reports about who's sending as your domain.

```txt
v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourcompany.com
```

Start with `p=none` to monitor, then move to `p=quarantine` or `p=reject` once you're confident legitimate senders pass authentication.

## How they work together

| Record | Purpose | Where it lives |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| SPF | Authorize senders | DNS TXT |
| DKIM | Sign messages | DNS TXT + mail provider |
| DMARC | Policy + reporting | DNS TXT |

All three should pass for best deliverability. Many inbox providers now treat missing authentication as a negative signal.

## Quick checklist for small teams

1. Enable DKIM in Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 admin
2. Publish SPF including your ESP and workspace provider
3. Add DMARC with `p=none` and a reporting address
4. Review reports monthly before tightening policy
